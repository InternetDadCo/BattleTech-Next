# AI and the BT Community

There was, and continues to be criticism of this project on the grounds that it is AI-assisted. I should like to be very clear that the bulk of this criticism is appropriate, ordinary, well-placed, and is rooted in what I perceive to be the desire to preserve of what makes a community work. 

The BT modding community is very well established. It has been around for a long time and the hours of sweat and labor that have gone into it cannot be measured. Up until quite recently, if you were submitting a PR to an established mod, chances are that the team knew who you were and you had already demonstrated that you knew what you were doing. Quality control was built in to the process. 

Now, any idiot with a coding harness is suddenly an expert with access to pulling internal levers on projects about which they held no particular understanding a week before. This is a highly chaotic and destabilziing force, and if you care about preserving your community and the things that make your community strong and durable, you are not doing your job if you simply handwave in new and unproven contributors whose skill is as uncertain as their provenance and their longevity. All you know for sure is that they typed some stuff into a window and shipped some files. Your limited volunteer time cannot be redirected to reviewing every newly-minted vibe-coder with an agenda.

## "On the other hand..."

In my non-InternetDadCo life, I am a pilot. When aviation first became available to the masses, everybody who could get their hands on an airframe with wings and an engine did so. The result was a tremendous number of crashes, property damage, and a not-insignificant loss of life. Over time, we figured out how to manage the system: how to train pilots, how to certify parts. We came up with rules that governed the system which do gatekeep who may participate -- no way around that -- but better a disappointed and gate-kept wannabe pilot who gets grounded than a crash.

In the software world, the 'crash' metaphor requires no explanation, even if the blast radius is more of a nuisance than a physical harm. I think most people, by late 2026, understand that the genie is not going back in the bottle. "No AI" is rarely a hidebound insistence that we all go back to churning our own butter and knitting our own socks. It's an exasperated plea to add some guardrails, to hesitate before we let loose a destabilizing force, because fixing some bugs or saving a few seconds of load time isn't worth the loss of dedicated volunteers. AI can be great labor, but it's no substitute for the soul of a community.

That is precisely the sentiment with which I approached this problem in my real job, which concerns an application that has been around for more than 20 years and which has a dedicated community quite far from anything to do with BATTLETECH. We are a small team competing with companies many times our size, so we couldn't afford to sideline AI entirely, but we had a lot on the line. I saw parallels with this community (and many others besides) and very reasonable, smart people on projects like [MegaMek](https://megamek.org/) and [BTA](bta3062.com) adopting a hard stance against AI. Looking back, I saw that many of these stances originated in the Spring and Summer of 2026, or -- compared to today (mid-November) -- about three eternities ago. So I felt, in part, that I had to ingest the lesson that these established groups seemed very keen on, but also that the pace of change is so high that the right move in November is not necessarily the same as the right move in June. Certainly my own experience with this project reflects that: if I look back at what I was trying to get done on BT-UCU in June 2026, it was mostly frustration and annoyance that I wasn't getting anywhere. But that's one reason I work on this project: the obstacle is the way. If I could convert that frustration into progress, I might have a shot at doing the same thing "in real life" where the decision matters to my livelihood in a way that BT-UCU does not. You're all really swell, but it doesn't bother me if you don't participate in the project or use BT-UCU. I did this for me and for my kid and it's already been very rewarding.

That is why I deliberately chose this project as an outlet for what was, you will recall, nothing more than experimentation and a long-shot attempt to impress my child. It was (and remains) my judgment that nobody could have, much less would have made this attempt without AI assistance, so I saw a middle path: respect the long-established mod community's preferences _for their mods_ but supply a conservative, focused effort to address some long-standing community complaints.

## It's Up to Us

If we do our job correctly, then we can offer a reason to be an exception to the rule. We aren't fighting an 'AI vs. no AI' war with anyone and I am not trying to change anyone's mind. I am simply trying to create an example of a job done correctly, just as I am in my professional life, where doing so serves the customers who pay our bills just as UCU might serve the community. To that end, the vision and mission for this project is tightly scoped and the handful of concerns that have been raised, whether about AI or our installation process, will be addressed at the earliest opportunity.

I should simply like to be very clear that I am a volunteer, just as many in the mod community are, and I've sunk many, many hours and a not insignificant amount of money on licenses and compute for this project. I have already achieved my iniital aims, and I don't want to fight with anyone or try to get them to change their ways. Either UCU will prove itself or it won't. I hope you'll help us get to the finish line.
